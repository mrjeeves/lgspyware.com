Where it's happening

Two devices, two sets of evidence, gathered by different people.

The living room · their work

The television

Gamers Nexus, Level1Techs and two researchers · Sep 6 · 135 minutes

  • Maps the house: devices never paired with the set — phones, watches, thermostats, PCs
  • Maps the street: neighbouring Wi-Fi names, signal strengths, channels
  • Plaintext transcripts from audio captured with the screen off — the finding LG denies
  • Pulling it off the internet doesn't stop the capture; it stores what it gets and uploads on reconnect
  • Unpatched remote code execution in webOS, no CVE published

The desk · our bench

The monitor

Critical Error Computing · one machine, held uncleaned

  • Windows Update delivers two LG packages signed as drivers with no driver code
  • SYSTEM installs LG's app 32 seconds later — no window, no EULA
  • It outlives the monitor: still self-updating nine months after the panel was unplugged
  • Uninstalling the app leaves both packages resident and re-arming
  • LG promised a consent prompt on Aug 3. Nothing has shipped.

How it got here

Forum posts nobody acted on in 2024, a pop-up that made people look in July, and a 135-minute investigation this month.

    A user documents it installing with the blocking policies already enabled.

What to do about it

Two different jobs, both quick.

On the PC

Check, then remove

  • Search installed apps for LG Monitor and McAfee — either one you didn't install means you were hit
  • Remove the app and both driver-store packages, or it re-arms on the next listed panel
The two-step removal →
On the TV

Turn it down

  • Kill Live Plus — that's the content-recognition switch — and limit ad tracking
  • Turn off far-field voice if you don't use it; the network scanning stays either way
